Mini Drone Gimbal v1.2

A printed three-axis test rig for your egg-bodied quadcopter, sized from your ruler photo: 140 mm edge to edge and 85 mm between motors. The frame is 298 mm wide and 264 mm tall, plus about 45 mm for the slip ring, with 20 mm of clearance round the props inside the inner ring. The rings print whole on an A1, and the frame bolts together from sections.

Assembly

  1. Print bearing_fit_test first. It has seats of 6.0, 6.1, 6.2 and 6.3 mm, marked by 1 to 4 notches. The default for the rings is 6.1 mm (2 notches). If another seat grips better, change BEARING_D and regenerate.
  2. Bolt the frame sections together. Join the four base sections and the two top bar sections with M3 × 20 screws and nuts.
  3. Press the bearings into the pads of both rings and the base. Push on the outer race only. The top bar has no bearing of its own.
  4. Fit the cradle inside the inner ring with two M3 × 8 screws through the bearings, with a shim under each head. Push an M3 nut into the slot in each arm first, and hold it there as the screw starts. Tighten until the shim just clamps the inner race, then check it spins.
  5. Fit the inner ring inside the outer ring the same way, with a nut in each boss slot, then check it spins.
  6. Screw the uprights into the base from underneath, between the ribs. Hold the outer ring in the frame with its posts at top and bottom, drop the top bar on and fix it with two M3 × 12 screws.
  7. Fit the last M3 × 8 screw, shim and nut through the base. Screw the base to the bench.
  8. Seat slip_mount with its spigot in the top bar's hole. Line up the two foot holes with the two 2.6 mm holes in the top of the bar, then fit two M3 × 16 screws. They thread into the plastic. Press the 6702ZZ bearing into the pocket inside the mount from above, pushing on its outer race only, until it sits on the shelf.
  9. Choose the rotor wires for power, trim and insulate the rest, and thread the power wires down the centre of slip_tube so they come out of its narrow end.
  10. Drop slip_tube down through the bearing, mount and top bar, feeding the wires ahead of it, until its narrow end seats in the centre hole of the outer ring's top boss and its shoulder rests on the boss. Pull the wires through the slot in the ring's inner face and glue or tape them along the ring, off to one side of centre.
  11. Turn the tube until a dimple lines up with the side hole in the boss, then fit the M3 × 5 side screw. Don't use a longer screw, because it would reach the wires.
  12. Lower the slip ring's stem into the tube and fix the flange with three M3 × 8 screws and washers. Let the outer ring sit on its bottom bearing, then tighten the two set screws gently and evenly through the mount's side openings, so they don't pull the stem off centre. Spin the ring by hand: if it feels tight at one point, loosen the set screws and tighten them again.
  13. If you are using a sensor, fix the board to sensor_mount with foam tape or an M3 in a slot. Put a nut in each pocket on the underside, stand the legs against the underside of the tray and fit two M3 × 16 screws down through the tray. The notch marks the +Y end.
  14. Stick the foam pad inside the crown of cover. Pierce it through the 1.5 mm centre hole if you need the hole clear.
  15. Sit the egg in the round seat with its long axis along the arms, which run between the front props and between the rear props. Lower the cover over the egg so each drone arm sits in a notch.
  16. Lay clip between the two ribs on the cover and press straight down until both legs snap under the tray. Each leg has twelve notches, 4.5 mm apart, and the tray edge can sit in any matching pair. Start with the eighth pair up from the feet. If the drone rattles, press the clip further down to a shorter pair. If the clip won't reach, use a longer pair nearer the feet. Use the same notch on both legs, counting from the foot.
  17. To take the drone out, pull the two thumb tabs apart and lift the clip off.

Slip ring, updated in v1.2

How it works. The top bearing and screw are gone, so the slip ring's own bearings now form the top pivot. Its body stays still on slip_mount, while its stem turns with slip_tube and the outer ring. The bottom pivot still carries the ring's weight.

New in v1.2. A 6702ZZ bearing inside slip_mount steadies the tube just below the slip ring, so the slip ring's stem takes less side load. Upgrading from v1.1 only needs the new slip_mount and the bearing. Your printed tube, top bar and outer ring all stay.

Already printed your parts? Reuse the top bar: remove the bearing, cut away the thin floor of the 19 mm hole, and drill two 2.6 mm holes 13 mm deep in the top face, 24 mm either side of centre. Reprint the outer ring, because the top boss no longer has a nut slot or nub, and it now has the centre hole and side screw hole.

Measure before printing. The sizes come from a similar 22 mm slip ring's drawing, and your photo's proportions match it. Check these on your own ring and change the SLIP_ values if any differ.

Current. Each ring is typically rated at 2 A, with thin 28 AWG wires. Use three rings in parallel for positive and three for negative. Wire and contact resistance will still drop some voltage, so keep the wires short, and never let the drone's supply go above 4.2 V.

Only yaw spins freely. Past the outer ring, the wires must cross the pitch and roll pivots, so those axes need a soft loop of thin silicone wire and can't turn continuously. Stop and untwist if the loop tightens.

Before you fly it

Balance the drone first. The axes pass 20 mm above the tray, and the drone's arms sit about 8 mm above it, so the drone hangs roughly 12 mm below the axes. With props off, the drone should stay at any angle you leave it. If it swings back to level, its centre of gravity is too low, so raise it on foam tape.

The rig adds mass. The rings, cradle, cover and clip add up to about 90 g, including the sensor mount but not the sensor. Check that against your drone's weight, because a small drone will swing them slowly. Use the rig for checking motor order, direction and rate response, with a gentle tune.

Drone size. The 140 mm tip-to-tip size comes from your ruler photo. Motor spacing, prop length and egg width and length are read from the same photo, so expect a few millimetres of error. The 30 mm body height is measured. How far the egg drops into the seat is worked out from its shape, the arm, prop and motor heights are read from a side photo, and the centre of gravity is an estimate.

Checked so far. A swept collision check passes on all three axes against the real prop sweep, with a smallest gap of 0.3 mm where the screw heads sit in the bearing holes. The slip ring tube turns in a 6702ZZ bearing inside its mount, and clears the rest of the mount by 0.8 mm and the top bar by 2 mm, and the side screw head clears the frame on a full yaw turn. The props clear the inner ring by 20 mm, the outer ring by 13 mm and the frame by 20 mm at their worst angles. Nothing has been printed or spun yet, so bearing fit and friction are still unproven.
